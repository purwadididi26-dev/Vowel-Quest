Learn: Phonetics Basics

Open each section in order. Explanations are simplified for learners; some short Indonesian notes are included (catatan singkat dalam bahasa Indonesia).

1. Introduction to IPA

The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) is a set of symbols where each symbol stands for one sound. We use it because English spelling is not a reliable guide to pronunciation.

Example: “see”, “sea” and “key” are spelled differently but all have the vowel /iː/. In contrast, “read” can be /riːd/ or /red/ with the same spelling. Symbols in slashes // show the sounds of a word.

IPA menunjukkan bunyi, bukan ejaan. Satu simbol = satu bunyi.

2. Understanding Vowel Sounds

A vowel is a sound made with a fairly open mouth, with no blocking of the air. We describe vowels in three ways:

  • Tongue height: close (high tongue, as in /iː/) or open (low tongue, as in /ɑː/).
  • Tongue position: front (/iː/), central (/ə/) or back (/uː/).
  • Lip rounding: rounded (/uː/) or unrounded (/iː/).

Close = lidah tinggi; open = lidah rendah; front = depan; back = belakang.

3. Monophthongs

A monophthong is a vowel with a relatively steady quality: the tongue and lips stay in roughly the same position. This model has 12: /iː/ see, /ɪ/ sit, /e/ bed, /æ/ cat, /ɑː/ car, /ɒ/ hot, /ɔː/ law, /ʊ/ book, /uː/ food, /ʌ/ cup, /ɜː/ bird, /ə/ about.

4. Diphthongs

A diphthong glides from one vowel quality to another within one syllable. The tongue and lips move. This model has 8: /eɪ/ day, /aɪ/ my, /ɔɪ/ boy, /əʊ/ go, /aʊ/ now, /ɪə/ near, /eə/ hair, /ʊə/ tour.

Academic note: this site uses the traditional RP teaching inventory. Modern British English varies, and /ʊə/ is not a distinct diphthong for all contemporary speakers (many say /ɔː/ in “tour” or “sure”). American English uses a different vowel inventory and is not taught here.

Diftong: bunyi vokal yang “bergeser” dalam satu suku kata.

5. Minimal Pairs

A minimal pair is two words that differ in only one sound and have different meanings. Examples: ship /ʃɪp/ and sheep /ʃiːp/; bed /bed/ and bad /bæd/; full /fʊl/ and fool /fuːl/.

Changing one vowel changes the word, so that vowel is a separate phoneme. If you mix them up, listeners may misunderstand you.

6. Pronunciation Tips
  • Listen: play a word several times before speaking.
  • Compare: say both words of a minimal pair and notice the difference.
  • Practise: record yourself and compare with a verified recording.

Common mistakes:

  • Using the same vowel for /ɪ/ and /iː/ (ship and sheep). Make /iː/ longer and tenser.
  • Making /e/ and /æ/ the same (bed and bad). Open your mouth more for /æ/.
  • Turning diphthongs into short vowels (say /əʊ/ in “go”, not a plain “o”).
  • Pronouncing “r” after vowels in car /kɑː/ or bird /bɜːd/. Traditional RP does not.
  • Rounding /ʌ/ in “cup”. It is unrounded.

Indonesia punya lebih sedikit kontras vokal, jadi sebagian bunyi Inggris terasa baru. Latihan mendengar sangat membantu.
