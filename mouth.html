Mouth Position Guide

How to use: open each section, then look at the diagram. Vowels are described by tongue height, tongue position (front to back) and lip rounding. These diagrams are simplified learner sketches, not precise measurements.

1. Tongue height: close, mid, open

Close vowels (/iː/, /uː/) have the tongue high, near the roof of the mouth. Open vowels (/ɑː/, /æ/) have the tongue low and the jaw more open. Mid vowels (/ə/, /ɜː/) are in between. Textbooks also use close-mid and open-mid.

2. Tongue position: front, central, back

It describes where the highest part of the tongue is. Front: /iː/, /e/, /æ/. Central: /ə/, /ɜː/, /ʌ/. Back: /ɑː/, /ɔː/, /uː/.

3. Lip rounding

Lips can be spread (/iː/), neutral (/ə/) or rounded (/uː/). Among the monophthongs, /ɒ/, /ɔː/, /ʊ/ and /uː/ are rounded. Others are unrounded. /ɑː/ is unrounded even though the mouth is wide open.

4. Diphthongs: moving between positions

In a diphthong the tongue (and often the lips) moves within one syllable, for example /əʊ/ moves from central towards back and from neutral to rounded lips. See the arrows in the Vowel Library.

Academic note: descriptions vary between textbooks, and modern RP differs from older descriptions (for example /uː/ is often more central, and /ʊə/ is not distinct for all speakers).
